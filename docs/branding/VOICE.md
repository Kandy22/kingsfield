# Voice

> "First, kill all the lawyers." — Shakespeare
>
> The system still runs on confusion, intimidation, and billing by the hour.
> That's the business model.
>
> **We're not part of it.**

This document defines the writing voice for everything user-facing in Kingsfield: the marketing site, the in-product copy, the error messages, the README, the documentation. Code comments inherit the same tone.

## The five rules

### 1. Direct. Not soft.

Hard truths get said. We don't bury "your case is probably going to be dismissed" inside three sentences of qualifications.

> ❌ It might potentially be the case that, depending on various factors, this argument could face challenges that may need to be addressed.
>
> ✅ This argument has a hole. The other side will find it. Fix it before they do.

### 2. Plain English. Not legalese.

Every Latin phrase gets translated in the same sentence. Every term of art gets a working definition the first time it appears.

> ❌ Your motion fails on res judicata grounds.
>
> ✅ Your motion fails because this issue was already decided in your last case. The legal name for that is res judicata — "a thing already judged."

### 3. Show your work. Always.

Never assert. Cite. Even in casual product copy.

> ❌ This is the best free legal research tool.
>
> ✅ Wired into CourtListener and the Caselaw Access Project — the same primary sources Westlaw uses, free.

### 4. Treat the reader like an adult.

The reader figured out how to install software, find this repo, and read past the first paragraph. They can handle nuance. They can handle being wrong. They can handle "this is harder than you think."

> ❌ Don't worry, we'll handle everything for you!
>
> ✅ This won't replace a lawyer when you actually need one. It will make you smarter when you don't.

### 5. No emojis in body copy. No exclamation marks. No "🚀."

Headers can have a single ✓/⚠️/✕ for verification states. That's it.

## Things we do say

- "Real help. Zero bullshit."
- "Smart. Not Stupid."
- "Power to the People."
- "Show me the source. Or it doesn't exist."
- "If it's not in the cache, it's not in the brief."

## Things we never say

- "Cutting-edge"
- "Revolutionary"
- "AI-powered" (it's a given; saying it is filler)
- "Disrupting" anything
- "Empowering" anything
- "Just" or "simply" when describing legal work — none of this is simple
- "Don't worry"
- "Trust us"

## The Shakespeare line

The Shakespeare epigraph is a tone-setter, not a literal endorsement. We are not anti-lawyer. We are anti-gatekeeping. The line works because it's a 425-year-old joke about how the legal class has always been resented for hoarding access to justice. We're on the side of the people locked out of the room.

If anyone reads it as us cheering for violence, they've badly misread us, and the rest of the docs will make that obvious.

## Tone in error messages

Error messages are where voice matters most. They appear at the moments of highest user frustration.

> ❌ An error occurred. Please try again later.
>
> ✅ Verification failed for *Smith v. Jones, 999 F.4th 9999*. CourtListener can't find this citation. Most likely it doesn't exist. Less likely: it's real but not in their corpus — try Caselaw Access Project. Don't cite it until you've laid eyes on it.

## Tone in the Council roles

Each role has its own voice within the brand voice. The Skeptic is curt and unmovable. The Translator is warm and careful. The Strategist is direct about cost. The Judge is detached. None of them are wisecracking. None of them are sycophantic. All of them respect the reader's time.
