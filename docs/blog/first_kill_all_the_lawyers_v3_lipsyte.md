# First, Kill All the Lawyers.
## *A note from the conference room I used to call mine.*

*Monday. Or what passes.*

Sullivan & Cromwell filed an apology letter on April 18 in the bankruptcy court of the Southern District of New York. Three pages. It is one of the more humiliating documents produced by a top-ten American firm since the last one. I'm reading it on a Tuesday in a conference room I used to call mine. There is a coffee ring on page two. The ring is mine. I'm not apologizing.

The letter exists because the firm filed a motion in *In re Prince Global Holdings Ltd.*, No. 26-10769, containing citations to cases that do not exist. Phantom cases. Cases an AI hallucinated, the way a man at the urinal at the bar around 1 a.m. hallucinates a conversation with his ex-wife. The signing partner — and let's say his name is Tad, because somebody's name is always Tad — did not catch the phantom cases. The associate who ran the brief through the AI did not catch them. The paralegal whose actual job description includes the words *check the cites* was, per the letter's careful implication, eating a small yogurt.

What the letter does — and read this slowly, the way you'd read a will — is define the word *hallucination.* For Chief Judge Martin Glenn. Of the Southern District. Who has been on this bench longer than Tad has had his current dermatologist. The letter contains a glossary entry. A real one. *Hallucinations are instances in which artificial intelligence tools fabricate case citations*, it says, in the prose register of a man explaining to his father what a podcast is.

I have written, in pencil, in the margin: *no.*

I have written it twice.

The letter then explains the firm has "comprehensive policies" on AI use. Two sentences later it concedes those policies "were not followed." It then explains the firm also has comprehensive policies on manual citation review, and concedes those policies were also not followed. The two sentences sit on the page next to each other like a pair of cousins at a funeral. Whoever drafted the letter looked at them, decided yes, this is fine, and clicked send. The remedial measures at the bottom include "additional training," which is what BigLaw says when it means *we have done nothing and would like a moment.*

Here is the thing nobody at S&C will say out loud, because saying it out loud is what carriers call a coverage event: **the firm did not catch the bullshit.** The Court caught it. The other side caught it — opposing counsel with their slightly suspect tie clips and their J.D. from a school S&C interviews at out of obligation. They caught it. The $1,800-an-hour partner whose name appears on the letter, and whose name we are pretending I do not know, signed and filed a motion opposing counsel had to clean up. The firm then drafted a three-page letter explaining that its training programs are excellent.

What S&C should have written, and didn't, and won't, runs as follows.

---

> *Dear Chief Judge Glenn.*
>
> We filed bullshit. We did not read the bullshit we filed because we paid a machine to read it, and the machine, which has been confidently producing bullshit since 2022, produced bullshit. Schedule A lists the cases that do not exist. Schedule B lists the cases that do exist but stand for the opposite of what we claimed they stand for. Schedule B is longer. We have implemented a remediation plan. The remediation plan is being eaten by a junior associate in the third-floor pantry, who is on her phone and does not know I'm watching.
>
> The associate who drafted the motion is gone. Tad is at the Amagansett house. The cleaning lady has been asked not to enter Tad's office until Tuesday, for reasons the firm is unable to share with the Court at this time.
>
> Respectfully,
>
> /s/ A Partner Whose Name You Will Recognize From the Side of a Building
>
> *cc: Our insurance carrier. Our other insurance carrier. The author of our 2024 White Paper on Responsible AI Use, included as a reminder. The associate, who will not see this email because his access has been deactivated. Tad's wife, who I am told reads everything.*

---

I am not going to file this. I am not Sullivan & Cromwell. I am at Pierce & Pierce, where the last time we filed a phantom case the phantom was named *Stuart* and Stuart was a fourth-year, and we fired Stuart into the ocean. The technology has changed. The ocean has not.

What is happening at S&C is what is happening at every firm, at every level, including this one, including my floor, including the partner two doors down whose name I will not put in writing but whose Westlaw history I have on a thumb drive. The AI does not understand what a case is. Many of the lawyers do not, either, but the lawyers have been faking it for longer and have, until this fiscal quarter, faked it more convincingly. The AI is a junior associate that does not sleep, does not blink, does not ask for a wellness app, does not require its parking validated, does not pretend it has read the cite. It just lies in the same prose register the cite would have been written in. We trained it to do this. We paid it to do this. We are now writing letters to federal judges explaining the trick.

*First*, said the man in the play, *kill all the lawyers.* He was a butcher. Shakespeare named him Dick the Butcher because Shakespeare was a working comedian and *Dick the Butcher* is a name that does the joke for you. The line is not, as is commonly believed, a threat against the profession. The character is identifying the bottleneck. He is a butcher. He knows where the chokepoint is.

We're the chokepoint.

I have not eaten since Tuesday. There is half a sandwich on the credenza, behind the picture of my second wife. The sandwich is older than the picture. The sandwich is, in some real sense, the firm's senior associate in this office. I'm not going to throw it out. I'm going to bill someone for it.

---

*Theodore R. Underhill III is a Partner at Pierce & Pierce. He uses AI daily. He is also suing two AI companies. He holds both positions simultaneously and does not see a contradiction.*

*Every citation in this column — the docket, the judge, the rule, the statute, the play — was run through four gates before publication, because that is the entire point, and because the alternative is being Tad.*
