Session Query · Henderson v. Apex

"What's the strongest pretext argument under Patrick v. Ridge given a four-month-old performance review that says 'ready for promotion'?"

Matter · HendersonAsked · 2m agoRound · 1 of 1

The Five Seats

Seat 01

The Contrarian

Finds the holes. Argues the other side.
Claude Opus 4.6 ● Dissent — partial

The pretext argument is real but overstates the leverage from one phrase. "Ready for promotion" in a performance review is consideration-stage language, not selection-stage. Apex will frame it as "qualified to be evaluated," and the Fifth Circuit has credited that framing where interview notes back it. Risk: if Apex produces contemporaneous panel notes specifying "leadership presence" criteria, the pretext theory weakens substantially.

Seat 02

First Principles

Strips to constitutional + statutory bones.
Gemini Pro ● Concur

Title VII text prohibits discrimination "because of" a protected characteristic. Patrick exists because subjective criteria can be a vehicle for the very causation Title VII forbids. The Statute's burden-shifting structure (codified at 42 U.S.C. § 2000e-2) anticipates exactly this asymmetry — employer holds the documentary record, plaintiff holds the inference. The January review shifts the documentary record toward plaintiff.

Seat 03

The Expansionist

What else does this argument unlock?
Claude Opus 4.6 ● Concur — with addition

Pretext is the right frame, but don't stop at Patrick. Layer in cat's paw analysis if the interview panel deferred to a single biased decision-maker — that opens vicarious liability even if the panel itself was facially clean. Also flag potential disparate impact if "leadership presence" is a recurring metric Apex uses across promotion decisions. Discovery should pull every promotion decision in this dispatch group for the past 3 years.

Chairman's synthesis

The Council is 4–1 in favor of the pretext theory under Patrick v. Ridge, with the Contrarian flagging a real but addressable risk: Apex's potential production of contemporaneous interview-panel notes specifying "leadership presence" criteria. The discovery sequence must front-load the request for those notes.

If the notes don't exist or were drafted post-hoc, the pretext argument tightens to near-summary-judgment-proof. If they exist and have substance, the case is harder but still viable on the comparator analysis under Lee. Expansionist's recommendation to layer in cat's paw and reserve disparate impact for amendment is sound and adopted.

Proceed. The Contrarian's dissent is preserved on the record for the Strategist's draft.
