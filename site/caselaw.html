Patrick v. Ridge

Court: 5th Cir. Decided: Dec 21, 2004 Panel: King, C.J.; Higginbotham, J.; Dennis, J. Author: King, C.J. Treatment: Good law as of May 2026

Before King, Chief Judge, and Higginbotham and Dennis, Circuit Judges.

HEADNOTE. An employer's reliance on vague, subjective criteria — such as "leadership presence" or "good fit" — to justify an adverse employment action may, where the criteria are unmoored from contemporaneous documented performance, support a Title VII plaintiff's showing of pretext at summary judgment.

I. Background

Appellant Patrick brought an action under Title VII alleging that the Department of State's denial of a promotion to a Foreign Service position constituted unlawful race discrimination. After a period of documented satisfactory performance, including a written evaluation describing the appellant as "highly capable" and "promotion-eligible," the agency selected a candidate outside the protected class, citing the successful candidate's superior "judgment and presence in an interview setting."1

The district court granted summary judgment for the agency. We reverse.

II. Standard

Under the McDonnell Douglas burden-shifting framework, once a Title VII plaintiff establishes a prima facie case and the employer articulates a legitimate, non-discriminatory reason, the plaintiff may demonstrate pretext through evidence that the stated reason is "unworthy of credence."

III. Pretext and Subjective Criteria

We have long recognized that subjective hiring and promotion criteria carry a particular danger of masking discriminatory motive. While employers retain discretion to consider intangible qualities, the use of such criteria does not insulate employment decisions from Title VII scrutiny.

Where, as here, an employer's articulated reason rests on subjective evaluations that are not documented in any contemporaneous performance record, and where the record reflects affirmative documented evidence to the contrary — such as the appellant's "promotion-eligible" written evaluation — a reasonable fact-finder may conclude that the stated reason is pretextual.2

This conclusion is reinforced where the comparator outside the protected class possesses materially weaker objective qualifications. The asymmetry between objective record and subjective justification is itself evidence the jury may weigh.

IV. Disposition

The judgment of the district court is REVERSED and the case is REMANDED for proceedings consistent with this opinion.
