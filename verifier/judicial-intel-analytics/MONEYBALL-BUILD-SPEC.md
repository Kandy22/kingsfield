# Moneyball for Lawyers — Build Spec v0.1

Kingsfield judicial analytics architecture. Drafted 2026-09-06.

## 0. The thesis

Beane's edge was not better scouting. It was that on-base percentage was public, cheap, outcome-predictive, and unpriced while every front office chased the radar gun. The current judicial-intel pipeline leads with the radar gun: face, affect, and emotion signals from oral-argument video.

The unpriced stat in law is the **docket entry sequence**. It is public, machine-readable, enormous, and essentially unmodeled outside a handful of academic papers. Build that layer first. The behavioral layer becomes a tested increment on top of it, not the premise.

Stated as a falsifiable claim the project can win or lose on: *a shrunk, posture-aware docket-event model beats every commercially sold judge statistic, and the oral-argument behavioral layer either adds measurable AUC over it or it does not — and we will be the ones who find out.*

## 1. Data tiers, cheapest defensible first

**Tier 1 — RECAP bulk.** CourtListener publishes quarterly bulk CSVs generated by Postgres `COPY TO`, in the `com-courtlistener-storage` S3 bucket: courts, dockets, docket entries, opinion clusters, opinions, the citation map, parentheticals, judges, financial disclosures, and oral arguments. Marked free of known copyright restrictions. Regenerated on the last day of March, June, September, and December.

This replaces the 125-request/day API ceiling entirely. The API is for freshness and gap-fill; bulk is for the corpus. Nothing about the current architecture should be built against the rate-limited endpoint.

**Tier 2 — FJC Integrated Database.** Free, cleaned, case-level federal civil and criminal outcomes from 1979 to present, quarterly. The most underused asset in the field. Free Law Project already merged the civil IDB into CourtListener dockets (~28 fields: disposition, class-action flag, first-named-plaintiff county, and other case metadata), which means the docket-to-outcome join is already done for civil cases. Criminal and bankruptcy were not integrated as of that work; check current state before assuming.

IDB is also the **coverage control**. RECAP exists because somebody paid to unlock a document — its presence is not random. IDB is the population; RECAP is a biased sample of it. Any rate computed on RECAP alone without a check against IDB terminations is wrong in a direction you cannot sign.

**Tier 3 — PACER gap-fill.** Targeted fetches for dockets that matter and are missing, plus the community prayer system. Budgeted, not bulk.

**Tier 4 — State trial courts. This is the moat.** Over ninety percent of American litigation happens where no vendor has coverage, and that is exactly why it is defensible. Colorado runs ICON/Eclipse (28-year-old legacy, mid-procurement). Florida is clerk-by-clerk. Hard to get is the point; if it were easy it would already be priced. Sequence state acquisition *after* the federal model works, so state scraping is aimed at validated features rather than speculative ones.

**Tier 5 — Judge attribute layer.** FJC biographical directory, CourtListener people/positions/political-affiliations, judicial financial disclosures, state judicial performance evaluations (the Colorado OJPE material already collected), campaign finance for elected judges, reversal history. Attributes, never the headline product — see §7.

**Tier 6 — Oral argument.** The existing FL 2nd DCA corpus. Appellate courts are the right proving ground because they are the rare venue with both public video and clean outcomes.

## 2. Unit of analysis

Not judge → outcome. That is the vendor formulation and it is nearly information-free.

The primitive is the tuple: **(judge × motion class × procedural posture × movant type × counsel × opposing counsel × court-division × time)** → (disposition, latency).

Two consequences.

First, the dependent variable is not one thing. It is a *disposition* and a *duration*. Model duration with survival analysis, with pending motions right-censored rather than dropped. Everyone sells outcome classification. Nobody sells the time-to-ruling distribution, and time-to-ruling is what actually prices settlement leverage, client cash-flow risk, and calendar exposure.

Second, the docket is a sequence, not a bag of features. Order matters: a motion to dismiss filed before a scheduling conference is a different object from the same motion filed after two extensions and a discovery dispute. Sequence models over the event stream, once §3 produces the event stream.

## 3. The asset: the docket-event ontology

Entry text → typed event. This classifier is the actual intellectual property. There is no good open version of it. See the companion doc: *Docket Event Ontology v0*.

Three points that belong in the architecture rather than the ontology doc:

**Three clocks.** Every entry carries up to three dates: the filing date, the judge's signature date parsed from the text, and the clerk's entry date. Time-to-ruling must run signature-date-of-ruling minus filing-date-of-motion. Using the entered date silently bakes clerk lag into the judge's number. The gap between signed and entered is itself a clean court-administration metric nobody publishes.

**The motion→ruling edge.** PACER text carries explicit cross-references — "(Related Doc # 296)", "re: 21 MOTION", bare leading document numbers. Parsing these into edges is what makes the (motion, ruling, latency) triple computable at all. Parse coverage on that edge is the single most important pipeline health metric. Report it on every run; a model trained on a corpus where the edge resolves for 40% of rulings is a model trained on the easy 40%.

**Weak supervision is free here.** A large share of RECAP entries carry a `short_description` that is already a normalized event label — "Order on Motion to Approve Compromise or Settlement", "Order on Motion for Authority to Sell or Lease Property". Seed the label set from those, train on the raw description, apply to the entries that lack one. This is a few days of work, not a few months, and it beats hand-labeling.

## 4. Shrinkage is not optional

A judge with nine observed rulings on a motion class has no signal. Every product in this category quietly reports the raw rate anyway, and that is the field's standing scandal.

Empirical Bayes shrinkage toward the court-division mean, with the shrinkage weight driven by observation count. This is precisely the Moneyball statistical move — regression to the mean plus park factors, where the park factor is the court, the division, and the year. A judge's number should be reported with its own uncertainty and its own N, always, on the face of the product.

Corollary for the UI: never show a point estimate without its interval. A number that cannot be shown with an interval should not ship.

## 5. Metric stack v1

Named so a litigator understands each one in a sentence.

1. **JGR-s** — shrunk grant rate, by judge and motion class, with N and interval.
2. **TTR** — time-to-ruling survival curve, judge × motion class, censored for pending.
3. **UAT** — under-advisement tail, the 90th percentile of TTR. Sells itself to anyone who has waited nine months on a dispositive motion.
4. **BRP** — bench-ruling propensity, the share of rulings issued same-day or as text-only orders. A fast judge and a slow judge are different products for the same case.
5. **EXE** — extension elasticity: probability of granting an extension, and days granted versus days requested. Cheap, universal, never sold.
6. **AWAR** — attorney wins above replacement. The counsel fixed effect net of judge, court, nature-of-suit, and posture. This is the marketing hook and the hardest number in the stack; ship it last and ship it careful.
7. **SLR** — sua sponte rate: orders to show cause and sua sponte dismissals per case, split by represented versus pro se. This is the direct empirical test of the Scarlet Letter hypothesis and it falls out of the same ontology for free.
8. **Clerk lag** — entered minus signed, as a court-administration metric rather than a judge metric.

## 6. The real differentiator: causal, not correlational

Federal district assignment is random or as-if random within a division. That is a natural experiment sitting in public data.

It means the project can estimate a judge's **causal** effect on outcomes rather than a correlation confounded by case mix. Economists have used judge-leniency instruments for two decades; no one has productized the estimate. "This judge is fourteen points more likely to grant, identified off random assignment within division and year, with these balance tests reported" is a claim that survives adversarial reading, survives a motion in limine, and is publishable.

Publishable is the point: this is the same publish → conference → get-cited motion that the citation benchmark already runs, aimed at a bigger target.

Validation obligation: test balance of observable case characteristics across judges within court-division-year before claiming as-if randomness. Some divisions are not random (related-case rules, specialized dockets, single-judge divisions). Where randomness fails, say so and fall back to the descriptive number.

## 7. Sell calibration, not confidence

Brier score and calibration curves as first-class product surface. Time-based holdouts only — never a random split, which leaks the future into the past through case-level correlation.

"We said seventy percent and it happened seventy percent of the time" is a durable, defensible, checkable claim. A bare point prediction is a liability the first time it is wrong in front of a client.

## 8. Behavioral layer as experiment, not premise

The literature that survives scrutiny is interaction dynamics, not affect: question count per side, interruption rate, speaking-time ratio, latency to first question, prosodic arousal. Epstein/Landes/Posner on question counts and Chen/Halberstam/Yu on vocal characteristics are the honest citations; verify the reported effect sizes before quoting any number.

Face-emotion classification does not have the scientific standing to carry a claim (see Barrett et al., 2019, on the weak mapping from facial configuration to emotional state). Keep it as an unlabeled feature if at all; never as an assertion about what a judge felt.

The experiment: on FL 2nd DCA, where both video and outcomes exist, measure whether the behavioral feature set adds AUC over the docket baseline, out of time. If it does, that is the moat and the paper. If it does not, the finding cost 1,700 transcripts and saved the company a product line.

Note the generalization gap honestly: the validated results are appellate, mostly SCOTUS. Whether any of it transfers to state trial courts is unknown, which is precisely why it is worth owning.

## 9. Constraints that are also moat

**France.** Article 33 of the 2019 justice reform law criminalizes analytics keyed to judge identity, with a penalty attached. No EU sale of the judge-identity layer. Verify current text and enforcement posture before any international GTM.

**The US backlash vector.** France banned this because vendors productized judge *personality*. Product framing should stay on rulings, latencies, and procedural behavior — things the judge did on the record — and off temperament, affect, and inferred disposition. That framing is both more defensible and more useful, and it is what keeps the data access open.

**Biometrics.** Illinois BIPA, Texas CUBI, and Colorado's AI statute all bite on facial and voice processing. Judges are public officials acting in public proceedings, which helps; jurors are not, which is the actual landmine. Keep juror work at venire-aggregate level and off faces entirely. Several states restrict juror recording outright.

**Source terms.** Court video archives carry per-source terms of use, some restricting commercial reuse. Audit each channel before scaling the scraper, and keep the archival capture separate from the productized derivative — the archival instinct in the pipeline README is right, but archive and product need different legal footing.

## 10. Ninety-day sequence

1. **Days 1–15.** Pull RECAP bulk into Postgres/Supabase. Load FJC IDB alongside. Establish the coverage model: which dockets and entries exist, and how that correlates with case type, court, and party status.
2. **Days 15–35.** Ontology v0 to v1. Weak-label from `short_description`, train the entry classifier, parse the three clocks and the related-doc edges. Ship the parse-coverage dashboard first, before any model output.
3. **Days 35–50.** JGR-s with empirical Bayes shrinkage across the top motion classes. Calibration harness and time-based holdout in place from the first model, not retrofitted.
4. **Days 50–65.** TTR survival models, UAT, BRP, EXE.
5. **Days 65–80.** SLR and the pro se split — the Scarlet Letter test, which is also the LAFLA-facing artifact.
6. **Days 80–90.** The oral-argument lift test on FL 2nd DCA. AWAR design work begins; do not ship AWAR before the fixed-effect design is reviewed.

Checkpoint discipline: each stage produces a number with an interval and a coverage statistic, or it does not advance.

## 11. Open questions

- Criminal and bankruptcy IDB integration status in CourtListener as of now.
- Whether state judicial performance evaluation data can be joined to outcome data at judge level, or only read qualitatively.
- Whether AWAR is defensible at all given selection of counsel into cases — the attorney effect and the case-quality effect are entangled, and the honest version may require an instrument of its own.
- Whether the FL 2nd DCA outcome labels are clean enough for the lift test, or whether appellate dispositions need their own extraction pass.
