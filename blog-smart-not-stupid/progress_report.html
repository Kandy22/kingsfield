Six Weeks with Harvey, Two Hours with Madison, and One Critical Interface Question.

On the firm's AI integration strategy, the associate I've been calling the wrong name for four months, and why Harvey formats everything in Courier New now.

By Theodore R. Underhill III · 8 min read · Filed under AI Tools / BigLaw / Technology · Verified: No phantom citations this time

Thursday.

The firm adopted Harvey approximately six weeks ago. I know this because I was on the call where we announced it, I used the word "transformative" four times, and I billed the call at my standard rate to a client who I am confident will not read the invoice carefully enough to ask why their M&A partner was on a software orientation call for two hours on a Thursday afternoon.

I should say upfront that I understand Harvey completely. I want that on record. What follows is simply a documentation of the process by which I allowed a junior associate to walk me through certain interface elements as a mentorship exercise, because developing talent is something I care about deeply and also because my assistant was in Miami that week.

The associate is a third-year. Yale. Adequate. Her name is either Morgan or Madison; I have been calling her Morgan for four months and recently saw an email signature that suggested I may be wrong, but at this point correcting it would be embarrassing for both of us so we are simply going to continue.

The following is a reproduction of our correspondence. I have annotated it in the margins, as is my right as a named partner on the matter.

Matter Resolved

She arrived at three. I had Harvey open on my screen, which I want credit for. I had also, in preparation, minimized it and opened a WordPerfect document — which I use for first drafts because I have used it since 1987 and it has never once lost a document or suggested that I meant something other than what I typed.

Madison looked at the WordPerfect window the way my youngest looks at photographs of people using payphones. As if she understood what it was but couldn't locate the emotion appropriate to the moment.

WordPerfect 5.1

In active daily use · Pierce & Pierce, Partner Suite, 47th Floor

First released 1989. Still running. Courier New 12pt, default. Never lost a document. Has never autocorrected "hereinafter" to "herein after." Does not suggest synonyms. Does not want anything from the user. The gold standard.

Madison has seen it now. She is sworn to discretion.

The instruction to "be more specific" is something I found deeply ironic coming from a third-year who took eleven days to turn around a memo I needed in two. I typed "review this indemnification clause for a Delaware merger agreement and flag any non-standard carve-outs" exactly as she suggested. Harvey produced a thorough, well-organized analysis in approximately four seconds. I looked at it for a long time. I then typed "are you sure" and it said yes, essentially, with citations.

I did not tell Madison this.

Seventy pages.

does not need
this information.

I have since asked Harvey to format everything in plain text suitable for Courier New 12pt. The output is excellent. I am not going to tell the Boca client that the seventy-page agreement summary I sent him last week was produced in eleven minutes. He does not need that information. What he needs is the summary, which is accurate, thorough, and formatted in a font that communicates gravitas, which Calibri does not and never will.

I gave a presentation to the executive committee yesterday about the firm's AI integration strategy. I spoke for forty minutes. I used the word "transformative" six times, which is two more than the onboarding call, so the trend is good. I showed them a live Harvey demonstration. I typed the prompt exactly the way Madison taught me. It worked perfectly.

She watched from the back of the room. I did not introduce her.

Afterwards, the managing partner told me I was "ahead of the curve on this." I told him I'd been saying that for years. Madison sent me an email that said simply "Great presentation!" with an exclamation point that I have read several times and am still not sure about.

She's either going to make partner or leave for a boutique by spring. Those are always the two options with the good ones. I intend to do nothing to influence which way it goes, because in my experience doing nothing is how you keep your best people longest before they figure out they should leave.

Anyway. Harvey is excellent. I am very much on top of it.
